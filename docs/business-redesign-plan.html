Business redesign — places, equipment, breakdowns

Source: Desktop/Demo/features/ideas.html (the "Business design book"). This page says what I will change in RichLife to get there, in what order, and what I need you to decide first. Phases 1 and 2 are built with the recommended answers to §9; Gas Stations became the existing fuel-station entry (see the spec).

1. What the design book asks for

Five rules every business follows, applied to the 17 existing catalogue businesses plus a new one:

RuleBookRichLife today
BuyReal things earn — scooters, washers, treadmills, rooms, real car models.Generic assets ("Menu item", "Standard washer"), bought per business, income fixed or price × 0.0003.
PlacesOpen the same business in many places; each place has a street/area, its own level; busy areas earn and cost more.One business per catalogue entry, no location at all.
UpgradeUpgrade the place (size, capacity) and its equipment.One level per business (1–100, ×2 at 10/25/50).
BreaksA broken unit earns nothing until repaired. Repair, Fast repair, Repair all.Nothing ever breaks.
StaffWorkers per place; a manager keeps it running — and repairing — while away.A salary number and an employee count copied from the catalogue; manager = 4-hour offline shift.

Plus Gas Stations (Energy · Business Mogul · opens at $300,000) — new. The book also mentions weather, events, theft, inspections, stock-outs and mileage; see §8 for what I propose to leave for later.

2. The new model

Company
 └─ Business            (one per catalogue entry, as today — keeps level, manager, taxes)
     └─ Place           NEW  "24 Oak Street" · district tier · place level · capacity
         └─ Unit        NEW  "Bay 4 · Self-service bay" · income/s · OK | broken (what, since, repair price)

A Business stays the thing you open from the catalogue, so taxes, managers, business levels, the leaderboard and achievements keep working. Inside it, money is now made by units sitting in places.

Income

place income/s   = Σ (working units' income/s) × district multiplier × place-level multiplier
business gross/s = Σ place income/s                      ← replaces "base + assets"
netIncomePerSecond = gross × business levelMultiplier − salary/s   (unchanged formula after this line)

Everything above netIncomePerSecond — prestige multiplier, boost, offline, taxes — is untouched.

Districts

Each business type lists the place kinds it can open (park/beach/stadium for a food cart, downtown/station/airport for parking…). Each kind has a tier that sets cost and income:

TierPlace priceIncome ×Example
Quiet× 1× 1.0Suburb street, park
Busy× 2× 1.5Downtown, university, train station
Prime× 4× 2.2Airport, stadium, highway exit

Street names come from a server list (like manager names) — flavour only.

Place level

Level 1 → 10. Each level adds capacity (how many units the place holds: 1 → 6 bays, 20 → 200 spaces, 30 → 120 rooms…) and +5 % income. Price grows ×1.6 per level. Capacity is the real reason to upgrade: you cannot buy a 5th washer into a place that holds 4.

Breakdowns

  • Each unit type has a mean time between failures (e.g. a bike ~18 h, a WashTec tunnel ~60 h) and a list of named faults with a cost share and repair time — the same shape as Road to Rich's breakdowns.ts.
  • The server draws the next failure time breaksAt when a unit is bought or repaired, and sends it. The client never rolls dice: at breaksAt its ticker drops that unit's income, with no call needed. Offline income is integrated piece by piece the same way. The sync ceiling stays valid (breaks only lower income).
  • When the server sees a breaksAt in the past it picks the fault (seeded by unit id, so it is repeatable) and the unit shows "Pump failure · Repair $180".
  • Repair: pays the fault price, the unit is back after the repair time (1–36 h). Fast repair: × 2.5 price, back at once. Repair all: standard repair of every broken unit of the business in one call (one sync, one spend).
  • Repair money is a spend: not counted against allTimeEarnings, not part of totalValue.

Staff

Workers are derived, not hired one by one: each unit type says how many workers it needs (a hand-wash bay 2, an automatic tunnel 0.5, a taxi 1 per shift), and a place's salary = workers × the sector wage. That replaces the flat monthlySalaryCost and shows on the card as "4 workers". The manager keeps its 4-hour shift and gains one job: during the shift, a broken unit is repaired automatically (standard repair, paid from cash if cash covers it).

4. Backend (RichLife/)

  • Domain: Place and BusinessUnit entities inside the Company aggregate (under Business); BusinessAsset is replaced by BusinessUnit. Catalogue: PlaceKindEntry, UnitTypeEntry (evolves AssetCatalogueEntry), FaultEntry. New constants in GameConstants (tiers, place level growth, fast-repair ×2.5).
  • Income: Business.GrossIncomePerSecond becomes computed from places; offline accrual in Company integrates per unit between breaksAt boundaries and the manager shift; manager auto-repair runs there too.
  • Application: BusinessService gets open-place / level-place / buy-unit / repair / repair-all; DTO + mapper updates; achievements gain "first place", "10 places", "fix it" if you want them.
  • Infrastructure: EF configs, one migration — tables business_places, business_units, catalogue_place_kinds, catalogue_faults; data migration of every existing business (§6); new seed content (§5).
  • Tests: domain tests for income with broken units, piecewise offline accrual, capacity, repair/fast repair, auto-repair; endpoint tests for the new routes.

6. Existing players — nothing lost

  • Every owned business gets one place, "Main", Quiet tier, place level 1, with a capacity big enough for what it already owns.
  • Every owned asset becomes a unit in it with its current income and price, working, breaksAt drawn from deploy time (first failures land hours after deploy, not at once).
  • Today's base income (BaseIncomePerSecond) becomes a built-in unit of that place, so netIncomePerSecond is identical the minute after deploy.
  • Business level, manager, taxes, totalValue are untouched.

7. Frontend (idle-startup-frontend/)

  • Models in core/models/game.models.ts; GameService: new calls (sync first, as for every spend) and a ticker that drops a unit's income at its breaksAt.
  • Place card exactly like the book's mock: street + $/day, level bar, workers, units with OK / fault, one Repair button, one Upgrade button. Used in the manage-business dialog (which becomes a page per business: places list + "Open a new place").
  • Badges on the dashboard: "3 broken" per business; a "Repair all" action; toasts for new breakdowns and auto-repairs.
  • i18n en/fr/ar for every unit, place kind, fault and new server message; admin catalogue editor for places/units/faults.

9. Decisions I need from you

1. Business level vs place level.

Keep today's business level (players paid for it) and add place levels, or fold business level into places?

My pick: keep both — business level = brand multiplier, place level = capacity.

2. Auto-repair while away.

During a manager shift, may the game spend the player's cash on repairs? (Taxes never auto-charge; the bank does.)

My pick: yes, standard price, only if cash covers it — otherwise the unit stays broken.

3. How hard breakdowns bite.

A player who opens once a day would find most units broken. Mean time between failures 12–72 h? Or a softer rule: broken units earn 50 % instead of 0?

My pick: 0 % as the book says, MTBF 24–96 h, and the manager repairs.

4. Scope of the first delivery.

All three phases in one go, or Phase 1 + 2 first and look at it on the dev tunnel?

My pick: Phase 1 + 2, then playtest.

5. Gas Station numbers.

The book opens it at $300,000 but its cheapest station is $650,000. Fine as "licence $300k, then buy stations as places"?
