Slower game: P2 in 3–5 days, each level a bit longer

Numbers come from a simulator that plays the real catalogue like an active player (4 short sessions a day, managers rehired, 4-hour offline cap, saving for prestige, always buying the best deal). Real players are less optimal, so expect them to take a bit longer than this table.

Bug found (live now): business and place level-ups are priced from the opening price ($500 for a Delivery Company depot) but boost every vehicle inside — a $125 upgrade on 21 trucks pays back in seconds. An optimal player can reach P7 in about 1.5 days today. Fixed in this plan.

Each level takes 4.5 → 6 → 6.5 → 11.5 → 15.5 → 16 days for an active player: longer each time, never double.

What changes

  1. Level-ups priced from what is invested (the bug fix): a business level costs the larger of its opening price and 25 % of everything put into it, × 1.25 per level; a place level costs 25 % of the place and its units, × 1.35 per level.
  2. Each extra unit of the same type costs 20 % more than the last one in that business (the classic idle-game rule), and each extra place 40 % more. The first truck pays back fast; the 20th does not — so growth slows by itself.
  3. Lower incomes: the first unit of a type pays back in about 3 h at P1, 14–17 h at P2–P3, 1–4 days at P4–P7.
  4. Passive income 3/s → 0.1/s, and every new company starts with $1,000 so the first business opens at once.
  5. Prestige prices: P2 $100K · P3 $500M · P4 $2.2B · P5 $9B · P6 $30B · P7 $120B.

Money still counts every second. Managers, breakdowns, taxes, store and bank are unchanged.

Before I build it

1. Another reset?

New prices and incomes make old progress meaningless again. Players are a few hours into the new game, so I recommend one more reset (same rule as before: logins and company names kept).

2. The jump from $100K to $500M

The simulator needs a big P3 price because P1 businesses keep growing fast after P2. It works, but the number looks odd on screen. Fine as is, or should I rescale the P2 business prices so the prices grow more evenly (more work)?
